# What has this project learned?

Choose a question below. Each branch summarizes what its linked lessons can
help you decide. Read a matching lesson, then its source only when you need the
method, scope, or evidence. Reading every branch is not a startup step.

- [A particular theory](theories.md) — Find its mechanism, sub-theory lessons, failed approaches, and where to check current evidence.
- [Can this edge actually be entered?](topics/execution.md) — Executable prices, spreads, liquidity, early close, and entry timing.
- [What does the measurement establish?](topics/evidence.md) — Independent observations, corpus limitations, and what a study can support.
- [How do we avoid repeating operational work?](topics/operations.md) — Collection, recovery, and reusable repository constraints.
- [What changed across the project?](../RESEARCH_LOG.md) — Recent consequential findings and corrections, with links to their owners.
- [Why did earlier sessions decide that?](archive/README.md) — Historical narrative for targeted retrieval; past instructions may be superseded.

For current status, use `python -m tools.cli state`, `studies`, and
`tickets list`. The maps do not duplicate those records. Before an expensive
repeat, also search relevant original notes and the idea registry: this is an
initial curated map, not an exhaustive inventory of the archive.

To save a learning, follow [the memory policy](../docs/agents/research-memory.md)
and [lesson format](templates/lesson.md). One lesson has one home; a summary
links to it. Maps stay under 500 words and 12 entries by branching into more
specific questions as needed.
